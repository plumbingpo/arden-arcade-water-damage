Water Damage We See Most in Los Angeles

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Los Angeles, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Los Angeles can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. In Los Angeles we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Los Angeles jobs, with the better option for each.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Drying Out a Los Angeles Property: What to Expect

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. On each Los Angeles job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Los Angeles homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
